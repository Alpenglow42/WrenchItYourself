TFT Sunshade

The first thing I've done is I had a friend at work 3D print me a sunshield or shroud for the TFT display, they weren't selling them on AliExpress anymore. It worked perfectly and install was fine using the plastic rivets that were already there. Since then I've put some insulation foam on back to ease vibration at certain RPM.

Anti-Glare Screen Film

The next modification came about by wanting some good sunglasses, but polarized ones made it so I couldn't see the screen, and I couldn't find non-polarized ones I liked that would be good all-arounders. So as is often the case in motorcycling, one small problem leads to a financial chain of events to solve it. I ended up buying some polarizing film on Amazon, and using it to flip the light output so I could now see with glasses on. Not only did this work, it made the screen go from matte to glossy, and made it easier to see in direct sunlight with no sunglasses as well. Easy and surprising upgrade.

Custom Seat Modification

As I mentioned the seat sucked for longer rides, and also got hip flexor cramps as well. I'm only 5 foot 10, and seemed too short for me. So I couldn't find lowering pegs, so I got a cushion, knocking out two birds with one stone.

I got one tractor seat shaped 3D TPU gel seat with split down the middle. It was too big but did the job, however after about a month of riding it became flat and didn't do it for me. So I sent it back for one without a split and better trapezoid shape for my seat, this did work better, but I still didn't have the support I wanted.

So the issue was that I had soft on soft and just no support, what I needed was a custom seat, and looking at it, most of those start with denser supporting foam. This got me to thinking, could I just add a piece to get by. So I traced the cushion on old ¼ inch yoga pad 2x and put that under the cushion case, and it's worked beautifully for the last few months. I still may end up going custom but this works for now.

Auxiliary Lights

The last thing I did, as I started riding in the evenings more, was I added auxiliary lights to my bike's crash bars. I chose Auxbeam M1 because they seemed small, cheap, 100 bucks, and I had used their bulbs in my Toyota already. They have been working great, and install wasn't hard, although I had to get a Posi-Tap to wire it in, and learn my multimeter again, which isn't as scary now and seems rather useful. AI is good with helping explain that.
