CFMoto 650 ADVentura Custom Seat

From a $25 gel pad to the $300 fix.

← Back to CFMoto 650 ADVentura Mods

Short version: I spent over 60 bucks on gel pads and a yoga mat hack before paying about 300 for a custom seat. Just get the custom seat.

The Problem

The one thing I really found that I didn't like on the CFMoto 650 ADVentura / MT is the seat. The biggest thing is that it's too low, and the ergonomics gave me cramps in my hip flexors. On top of that, I started going on all-day, 8+ hour rides over the summer, and the seat was just hard and unsupportive.

The Cheap Fixes

First I got a 25 dollar 3D TPU gel pad, the tractor seat style. It wasn't so bad, but after an hour or so it was clear the design was just too big for the shape of a 650MT's seat. So I sent it back and got a trapezoid shape without a split in the middle. This was better, but I'd still keep sinking and feeling the hard seat pan underneath.

I had an old yoga mat, about 1/4 inch thick, so I traced the seat out on it. Two layers is what I settled on. This brought me up about 2 inches, and the stiffer yoga foam gave the setup a lot of support.

Now I had mostly solved the two biggest problems. I wasn't sinking into the seat pan, and I was sitting higher with better ergonomics, so no more hip flexor cramps. I rode this way most of the summer. But I didn't like two things: it looked a little janky, and it wasn't very stable, so I couldn't move around on it. It was a good enough fix for right now, but obviously not a permanent solution, and it still caused discomfort on rides past 2 hours. This is the place where a lot of people end up: a custom seat.

DIY or Take It to a Pro

There are two ways to approach this: DIY, or take it to a custom seat maker. I looked at DIY first. But there was the time, the chance of messing something up, and the cost of specific tools versus how often I'm actually going to do this. I also don't have a place to properly work on foam. I can wrench on my bike in a parking spot, but shaping foam is a different story. I would have loved to tackle this project, but time was the big one. I'm finishing grad school with a full time job.

So I contacted a local seat maker, Slims Seats in Lee's Summit, MO. We talked in August and he quoted me a range of 250 to 350, which I thought didn't sound so bad. But it was peak riding season and he was booked out 6 weeks. I waited until the weather was changing and contacted him again in October. This time I was quoted about 300, with about a week to complete it.

Picking a Design

Honestly, I didn't really know what I wanted. I watched endless YouTube tutorials on refoaming and progressive foam, versus people just adding foam or gel. And really the heart of everything is the shape. That dictates the look and what the cover will look like. So I started looking at custom seats for similar banana seat bikes like the Versys 650 and SV650, and found some designs I liked.

Once I had a general idea, I used Gemini to start generating some images. Classic pleated chevrons looked great, but I wanted something unique, different, modern, futuristic. And funny enough, I thought all those pictures of carbon fiber and fullerenes with their linked hexes looked great. For most people that's it, it just looks cool. But I'm a former research scientist, so to me it also represented carbon-carbon bonds and hexagonal carbon rings, like you see in benzene and graphene. Some of the strongest structures in nature.

All four concept images above were generated with Gemini.

Now to geek out a bit. Each corner of a hex represents a carbon atom. Carbon can form four bonds, and in a sheet like graphene each carbon locks onto three neighbors, with the leftover electrons shared across the whole sheet. That's what makes these some of the strongest bonds there are. See more here: Allotropes of carbon (Wikipedia).

For the sides I went with a classic carbon fiber look, which matches the fake plastic carbon fiber panels my bike already has. I settled on a design, passed it by the expert, and went down for a fitting.

The Fitting

At the fitting, Slim got to see how I sat on the bike and how my temporary setup worked. After measuring the height, we settled on raising it 2 inches by varying the foam height. My wife had also complained about her seat, and Slim suggested adding an inch there. He told me to go order a yard of whatever fabric I wanted, then bring the seat back and drop it off once I had it.

Finding the Fabric

This took a few days of searching. The pattern I really liked had white stitching that made the black vinyl hexes stand out. I ran it through Gemini and was like wow, that looks amazing. One problem: it was 68 to 98 dollars a yard. So I started searching for alternatives and found some foam backed black hex vinyl for 32 on Amazon. I ordered it and had it a week later.

The First Rides

I got the seat back and it looked cool, but I really wanted to see how it rode.

Being early November, the temperature was in the 40s, so I layered up under my mesh gear and took rain gear to block the wind. It wasn't that bad. I rode for around 5 hours, and man, I felt so much more confident on the bike. I felt like I was just riding hard over everything. This was a total surprise. I could move around better and check my blind spots better, and of course the ergonomics were better. The only thing I wanted was a bit more plushness. But I have to admit my butt wasn't sore or falling asleep.

Saturday I rode for around 6 hours, 2 of them continuous. The only thing I noticed was that the spot where my sit bones land could maybe use 1/4 to 1/2 inch more support. But I didn't want to make that call on just one ride. It needs to bug me more over time, so I can tell if it's a real issue or just a new seat I'm getting used to.

The One Fix

What did bug me was a wrinkle in the cover at the front. Slim asked for feedback, and this was all I really had. He said he could stretch it out no problem, bring it back whenever. So I brought it back, and about 20 minutes and a few torn out staples later, the wrinkle was gone.

I took the opportunity to ask Slim about making the seat wider, and whether that would need a new cover. He thought the cover could take it. I said I'd think more about it in spring, after more rides.

The Verdict

So that's it. It was a bit of a process, and along the way Slim gave me a few pointers on building a seat and cutting vinyl. I'm definitely happy with what I got. Maybe I'll need a few modifications, but that's ok, it's part of the process. It is definitely better than the fix I had, or getting one of those stupid Wild Ass cushions.

300 bucks. Just get a custom seat.
